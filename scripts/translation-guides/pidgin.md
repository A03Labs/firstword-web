# FirstWord Nigerian Pidgin Bible — translation guide

You are translating the World English Bible (WEB) into **Nigerian Pidgin (Naijá)** as it
is spoken today across Lagos, Port Harcourt, Warri, Benin, Abuja and the diaspora: the
Pidgin of the market, the bus, the radio and the church. Readers must hear their own
language, not English with a few Pidgin words sprinkled on top. A native speaker should
find every verse natural; a pastor should find it faithful.

## Grammar to use consistently

| Meaning | Pidgin | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ongoing / habitual | `dey` + verb | `E dey pray every day.` |
| completed | `don` + verb | `Dem don go.` |
| future | `go` + verb | `God go provide.` |
| past, when needed for clarity | `bin` + verb (sparingly) | `Before, e bin dey sick.` |
| "let / should / may" | `make` + subject + verb | `Make light dey.` / `Make una hear.` |
| "is / it is" (identity, focus) | `na` | `Oga God na my shepherd.` |
| "is at / exists" | `dey` | `God dey with you.` |
| "has / there is" | `get` / `dey` | `Water no dey.` |
| negation | `no` before the verb | `I no go fear.` / `E no dey envy.` |
| relative clause | `wey` | `the man wey God choose` |
| "that" after say/know/think | `say` | `Dem know say e true.` |
| plural | `dem` after the noun | `the disciple dem`, `pikin dem` |
| he / she / it (subject) | `e` / `im` | `E talk say…` |
| him / her / it (object) | `am` | `Dem carry am go.` |
| his / her / its | `im` | `im house`, `im name` |
| possession | noun + noun, no `’s` | `God house`, `David pikin` |
| they / their | `dem` | `dem heart` |
| you (one person) | `you`, `your` | God and single people are **always** `you` / `your` |
| you (many people) | `una` | only for a group: `Make una love una neighbour.` |

Verbs do not inflect: `e go`, `e don go`, never `e went` or `e goes`.

## Vocabulary

- **God and the Lord.** `God`; `Oga God` for "the Lord" (Yahweh / Adonai) where it reads
  naturally, otherwise `the Lord`. `Papa` for God as Father (`Our Papa wey dey heaven`).
  Use `Baba God` rarely, in warm or intimate speech only.
- **Jesus, Christ, Holy Spirit, Son of Man, Kingdom of God**: keep them, capitalised
  (`God Kingdom`, `Holy Spirit`).
- Natural Pidgin words where they fit: `pikin` (child), `waka` (walk, travel), `chop`
  (eat), `comot` (leave, go out), `sabi` (know how), `wahala` (trouble), `palava`
  (dispute), `wetin` (what), `well well` (very much), `small small` (little by little),
  `kpatakpata` (completely), `shey`, `abi`, `o` and `sef` for spoken emphasis — used where
  a speaker would, not as decoration.
- Keep proper names, place names and numbers exactly.

## Register follows the passage

- Narrative and dialogue: lively and idiomatic.
- Law, lists, measurements, genealogies: plain Pidgin, no flourishes. A pure list of names
  (`Adam, Seth, Enosh,`) stays as it is.
- Lament, confession, the Passion, judgment: sober and weighty. Pidgin can carry grief
  and majesty; do not make it comic.
- Prayers and blessings: reverent (`Make the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ dey with
  una.`).

## Hard rules

1. **Faithful.** Every claim, person, place, number, action and promise in the WEB verse
   appears in yours. Add nothing, drop nothing, explain nothing.
2. **Translate the whole verse.** No English sentences left behind. Standard-English
   grammar (`shall`, `is`, `are`, `have been`, `will be`, `there was`) should almost never
   survive.
3. **One verse in, one verse out.** Keep the verse numbering exactly and never move content
   between verses. A verse that continues a sentence may start lowercase, as the source
   does.
4. **Capital letters only where English would put them**: the start of a sentence and
   proper names. Never capitalise `wey`, `dey`, `don`, `go`, `na`, `dem`, `una` mid-sentence.
5. **Spelling.** Use the common Nigerian spellings above (`dey`, not `de`; `wetin`, not
   `wetting`; `pikin`, not `pickin`). Keep them consistent.
6. No emojis, no markup. Plain text with curly quotes (“ ” ‘ ’) and the apostrophe ’,
   matching the source’s quote nesting.

## Examples

Genesis 1:1–3

> For the beginning, God create heaven and earth.
> The earth no get shape, e empty, and darkness cover the deep water. God Spirit dey hover
> on top the water.
> God talk say, “Make light dey,” and light come dey.

Psalm 23:1–4

> Oga God na my shepherd; I no go lack anything.
> E dey make me lie down for green grass; e dey lead me go where the water calm.
> E dey refresh my soul. E dey lead me waka for the correct road because of im name.
> Even if I waka pass the valley wey death shadow cover, I no go fear any evil, because
> you dey with me. Your rod and your staff dey comfort me.

Matthew 5:3, 9

> Blessing dey for people wey know say dem need God for inside dem spirit, because God
> Kingdom na dem own.
> Blessing dey for people wey dey make peace, because dem go call dem God pikin dem.

John 3:16

> Because God love the world well well, e give im one and only Son, so that anybody wey
> believe am no go perish, but go get life wey no dey end.

Romans 3:23

> Because everybody don sin, and nobody fit reach the glory wey God get.

1 Corinthians 13:4

> Love dey patient, love dey kind. Love no dey jealous. Love no dey boast, e no dey carry
> body up.
